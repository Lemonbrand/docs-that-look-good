# How I want charts (lessons from the 24 charts we built for our inbox audit)

## Make a set, not one chart
- 3 to 5 charts. Each one answers a different question, written as its title.
- Cover different depths: the whole year (meaning), the month or season (decisions), one busy week (clarity). Most weight on the middle.
- Use different chart types for different jobs. All bars is the failure mode.
  - Share of the total: split bars or a treemap. Never a pie.
  - Change over time: a line, a slope chart, or stacked areas.
  - Ranking: horizontal bars, sorted, biggest first.
  - Peaks and patterns: a calendar grid (weeks by days) or a heatmap.
  - What adds up to the total: a waterfall.

## Make each chart read with no context
- Title = the question. Under the chart, one line with the answer ("Tax season doubles client questions in April").
- Label the bars and lines directly. No legend if there's room.
- Big text (nothing tiny). Never rotate labels: if they don't fit, turn the chart on its side.
- Group tiny slices ("3 smaller tasks") instead of drawing slivers.
- One accent color (green #2F6B4F) for what matters, grey #B8BDB9 for the rest. Lots of white space.
- A footnote with the source and the honest limit ("Logged hours only, calls not counted").

## Check before you show me
- Recheck every number and every sentence against the data. When we built ours, 4 of the written claims were wrong until we rechecked them.
- Look at every chart image: nothing cut off, nothing overlapping, no chart half empty.
- Never guess things the data can't show. Say what it can't see.

Deliver the charts as PNGs and one PDF page per chart with the title, the answer line and the footnote.
